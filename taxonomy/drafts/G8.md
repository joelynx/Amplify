## Decisions and open questions
- **Fluid Mechanics has three topics, not one.** About 1,750 questions over 14 chapter-sized areas would exceed 12 branches in a single topic. I used "Fluid Mechanics" (the core course, 11 branches), "Compressible Flow" (White ch. 9) and "Hydraulics and Hydraulic Machines" (open channels, pumps, turbines). If the owner wants the single "Fluid Mechanics" topic from the STYLE example, merge them by folding Compressible Flow into one branch and Pumps and Turbines into one "Turbomachinery" branch. That gives exactly 12 branches, with 13 subtopics in the compressible branch.
- **Heat Transfer is one topic, "Heat and Mass Transfer", with 10 branches.** Kern's process equipment (exchanger design, evaporators, waste heat boilers) has too few questions for a separate topic of 3 or more branches. It fills the "Heat Exchanger Design and Operation" branch and the "Evaporators" subtopic.
- **Thermodynamics is lean** (one topic, "Heat and Thermodynamics", 5 branches, 15 subtopics, about 105 questions). Several subtopics are core but small: Thermometry (~3), Rankine Cycle (~3), Refrigerators (~4), Specific Heats of Gases (~5). Possible merges: Thermometry into Calorimetry, and Rankine with Refrigerators into "Vapour Cycles". There is no Otto, Diesel or Brayton node, because no question outside the power-plant books needs one. The G9a draft already places Rajput's engine cycles in "Air-Standard Cycles for Engine Plants".
- **White's thermodynamics-review questions** (ch. 1 and 9: ideal-gas states, steam, humidity, specific heats, entropy change of a heated tank, isothermal compression work) go to Thermodynamics by main idea. Standard atmosphere, bulk modulus and speed of sound stay in Fluid Mechanics. Atmospheric pressure variation, including Kamal's isothermal atmosphere (21069) and Gnadig's lapse rate (16099), lives in Fluid Statics.
- **Rockets and resisted motion.** All of C87 goes to Mechanics, including White's ~10 rocket problems (3.100-3.109) and his projectile-with-drag ODE problems (18650, 18652). Terminal-velocity problems that use drag-coefficient data stay in Fluid Mechanics ("Terminal Velocity and Sphere Drag").
- **Speed of sound:** Fluid Mechanics keeps sound speed as a compressible-flow property. Kamal's derivations (Laplace formula, acoustic impedance) are wave physics and go to Mechanics.
- **Hydraulic turbines are named twice on purpose.** Rule: Rajput and Nag go to CET, which has "Pelton Wheel Hydro Turbines" etc. White's ~14 turbine questions stay in Fluid Mechanics as "Pelton Wheel" and "Francis and Kaplan Turbines", with scope notes pointing to CET. The owner may prefer one home.
- **Thermal expansion overlaps with Material Science.** The G9b draft has "Thermal Expansion of Solids" (~3) and "Thermal Stresses" (~5) from Callister ch. 19, and I have Kamal's "Thermal Expansion" (~9). It is the same concept (ΔL = αLΔT, constrained-rod stress). Recommend one home: either all in Thermodynamics (~17), or Material Science keeps only the material-property framing. Owner to decide.
- **Mass transfer versus diffusion.** Heat Transfer keeps Som ch. 12 (diffusion in gas and liquid mixtures, Stefan flow, evaporation) as "Steady Mass Diffusion". Material Science (G9b "Fick's First Law", "Fick's Second Law") keeps diffusion in solids and permeation through metals and polymers. Som 18520 (hydrogen through a plastic membrane) sits on the line.
- **Material thermodynamics** (Raghavan: entropy of mixing, Gibbs energy, rubber elasticity; C372) is left to Material Science, matching G9b.
- **Newton's law of cooling** (Kamal 21047, 21048) goes under Heat Transfer > Lumped Capacitance Method, not Thermodynamics.
- **Som ch. 6** (incompressible viscous flow review, about 30 questions: Couette, Poiseuille, boundary layers, turbulence MCQs) is filed in Fluid Mechanics by main idea, even though the source is a heat-transfer book.
- **Finite-difference methods** appear twice with different content: conduction (Heat Transfer) and Laplace/Poisson flow solutions (Fluid Mechanics, "Finite-Difference Methods for Flow Equations"). Neither belongs to a generic numerical-analysis node.
- **Non-Newtonian flow** has one home, "Non-Newtonian Fluids and Rheology" in Fluid Properties. It covers rheological models, rheometers and the pipe flow of power-law and Bingham fluids. "Viscometers" is for Newtonian fluids only.
- **Cavitation** has two names. "Cavitation" (Fluid Properties) covers flow over bodies, nozzles and venturis. "Net Positive Suction Head" (Pumps) covers pump suction.
- **Magnus effect versus circulation theory.** "Flow Past a Cylinder with Circulation" (Potential Flow) is the Kutta-Joukowski theory. "Magnus Effect" (External Flow) is experimental lift on spinning balls and Flettner rotors.
