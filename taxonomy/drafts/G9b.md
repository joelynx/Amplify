## Decisions and open questions
- Electrical Machinery has one topic, Electric Machines, with nine branches. Sen's "Transients and Dynamics" chapter is split into the machine branches: DC Machine Dynamics and Synchronous Machine Transients. The single induction-motor transient item goes under Torque-Speed Characteristic.
- Electrical Machinery vs Power Electronics: a question whose main work is converter analysis (firing angle, duty ratio, converter waveforms) goes to Power Electronics > Electric Drives. Motor-side speed control at a given voltage and frequency (V/f with an equivalent circuit, pole changing, Ward-Leonard) stays in Electrical Machinery. Servomotor control-loop items in C129 move to Special Machines.
- Electrical Machinery vs Material Science on magnetics: Electrical Machinery owns core-loss calculations, including Raghavan 5627, 5923 and 5924. Material Science owns B-H loops as material behaviour and soft and hard magnetic materials. No question uses Sen's permanent-magnet design (1.4), so permanent magnets live only under Hard Magnetic Materials.
- The per-unit system lives once, in Transformers. Power Systems should reuse it rather than duplicate it.
- Single-phase complex power and power factor (Chapman 1.9; 5940, 5941) are placed in Power Systems. This touches Electromagnetism and Special Relativity's "AC phasors". **Owner:** keep it here, or move it to the circuits subtree if that draft has an AC-power node.
- Power Systems is a one-branch topic, below the 3-12 guideline. Chapman and Sen contain no power-system analysis (load flow, faults, stability studies). A keyword search found no machinery, power or materials questions in the IIT exam papers, so nothing else feeds this topic. **Owner:** accept the thin topic, or fold AC Power and Three-Phase Circuits into a single Power Systems and Power Electronics topic.
- Left out because they have no questions (TOC-only): instrument transformers, transformer inrush current, harmonics in three-phase banks, amortisseur starting of synchronous motors, brushless dc motors, reluctance and hysteresis motors, two-wattmeter measurement, and Sen's wind energy chapter. Chapman 6078 (motor selection) is a wastebasket candidate.
- Source rules applied: Rajput and Nag items go to Conventional Energy Technologies, and Srinath items (16 laminates in C103, 2 in C139) to Mechanics. Kamal, Gnadig, Som, Kern, Schaschke and Gregory items in my clusters are filed by content into other subjects.
- Material Science has five topics, above the usual 1-3. The reason is size: about 1,050 questions here plus about 36 Callister items routed elsewhere, as large as a full mathematics subject. **Owner:** keep five, or fold Engineering Materials into the other topics (polymer and ceramic structure into Structure, their properties into Mechanical Behaviour).
- Material Science vs Mechanics: Mechanics keeps Hooke's-law and stress-transformation work (Callister 8044) and laminate theory (Srinath). Material Science keeps property extraction from tests: stress-strain curves, hardness, fracture toughness, fatigue and creep data. About 23 Callister items in C311 (outside my set) cover elastic stress-strain, fracture toughness, fatigue and three-point bend tests. They have homes here: Engineering Stress-Strain Curve, Fracture Toughness, Fatigue and S-N Curves, Mechanical Behaviour of Ceramics, and Resolved Shear Stress. Only the pure Hooke's-law items should go to Mechanics.
- Material Science vs Thermodynamics: Entropy of Mixing, Free Energy and Phase Stability, and Gibbs Phase Rule are kept here because the source and framing are materials (lattice sites, alloy phase diagrams). Thermal Expansion of Solids, Heat Capacity of Solids and Thermal Conductivity of Materials are named to separate them from general heat topics. Kamal's thermal-expansion items go to Thermodynamics. **Owner:** confirm that Thermodynamics will not also carry a phase-rule node. Also decide whether all thermal-expansion calculations should have a single home.
- Material Science vs Quantum Mechanics: free electron theory, the Fermi-Dirac distribution and Brillouin zones stay in Material Science, following the band-theory rule. Raghavan's uncertainty-principle and de Broglie items go to Quantum Mechanics. Electron configuration and periodic-table trends are kept in Material Science in their chemistry framing; Quantum Mechanics could claim the pure quantum-number counting items.
- Dielectric Materials is a thin, two-subtopic branch. Raghavan chapter 17 has no questions; the roughly 6 Callister items (capacitors, dipole moments) sit in C241 and C122, outside my set. It is kept because both books treat dielectrics as a core chapter.
- Glass transition: polymer Tg and Tm go in Polymers; glass-forming tendency of inorganic solids goes in Ceramics and Glasses. Precipitation is split the same way: the heat-treatment process is under Heat Treatment > Precipitation Hardening, and the mechanism under Strengthening Mechanisms.
- Raghavan 5324 and 5325 (arrangements of atoms on sites, in C123 outside my set) belong under Entropy of Mixing if the owner wants them in Material Science; otherwise Combinatorics.
- Names use British spelling (Behaviour, Fibre, Colour, Sulphur) to match the brief. Callister uses US spelling. **Owner:** choose one convention for the whole bank.
