# Subject: Combinatorics
## Topic: Enumerative Combinatorics
### Branch: Basic Counting Principles
- Sum and Product Rules — counting by cases and stages; strings, codes, functions between finite sets (~19)
- Permutations of Distinct Objects — ordered arrangements and k-permutations of distinct items, incl. adjacency demands (~11)
- Combinations and Committee Selection — unordered selections, committees, teams, splitting people into groups (~24)
- Permutations of Multisets — arrangements with repeated items, words from given letters, multinomial counts (~19)
- Combinations with Repetition (Stars and Bars) — identical objects into boxes; nonnegative integer solutions of x1+...+xk=n (~30)
- Lattice Paths — monotone grid paths, reading words in letter arrays; Dyck paths see Catalan Numbers (~13)
- Pigeonhole Principle — elementary guarantee questions (socks, gloves, birthdays); olympiad uses filed by content, tagged (~13)

### Branch: Counting with Restrictions
- Counting Subsets with Conditions — subsets or pairs of subsets with sum, size or intersection conditions (~36)
- Selections without Adjacent Elements — subsets or seatings with no two neighbours chosen, in a row or circle (~10)
- Counting Integers by Digit Conditions — how many integers have given digit properties; digit theory see Number Theory (~63)
- Counting Grid Fillings and Colourings — number of fillings or colourings of tables under row, column or neighbour rules (~26)

### Branch: Permutations and Symmetry
- Permutations with Restrictions — counting permutations with positional, adjacency or pattern conditions; excludes derangements (~37)
- Circular Arrangements — seatings and arrangements around a circle counted up to rotation (~7)
- Burnside's Lemma — counting colourings and labellings up to symmetry: necklaces, cube colourings (~12)

### Branch: Binomial Coefficients
- Binomial Theorem — coefficients and terms of binomial and multinomial expansions (~12)
- Binomial Identities — summation identities with binomial coefficients, by algebra or combinatorial argument (~53)
- Pascal's Triangle — row properties, parity of entries, monotonicity and bounds of binomial coefficients (~10)

### Branch: Inclusion-Exclusion
- Inclusion-Exclusion Principle — counting unions, Venn-diagram survey data, Bonferroni bounds; excludes derangements and surjections (~29)
- Derangements — permutations without fixed points, hat-check and ménage problems (~11)
- Counting Surjections — onto functions and distributions leaving no box empty (~5)

### Branch: Recurrences and Generating Functions
- Recurrence Relations for Counting — setting up recursions for words, walks, staircases; solving them see Algebra (~31)
- Counting Tilings of Strips — number of tilings of 2×n, 3×n boards or boxes; existence see Tilings and Dissections (~14)
- Generating Functions for Counting — ordinary and exponential generating functions, coefficient extraction, dice sums (~13)

### Branch: Special Counting Numbers
- Catalan Numbers — parenthesisations, triangulations, non-crossing chords, ballot sequences, Dyck paths (~19)
- Integer Partitions — partitions of n, Ferrers diagrams, making change, partitions with maximal product (~22)
- Stirling Numbers and Set Partitions — Stirling numbers of both kinds, Bell numbers, permutation cycles, involutions (~6)

### Branch: Counting Geometric Configurations
- Counting Figures in Grids — rectangles, squares, unit cubes and lattice points in grids and boards (~15)
- Counting Triangles and Polygons — triangles or polygons from point sets; integer-sided triangles of given perimeter (~19)
- Counting Diagonals and Intersection Points — numbers of diagonals, segments and crossing points in polygons (~11)
- Regions Formed by Lines and Curves — parts of plane or space cut by lines, circles, planes, diagonals (~17)

## Topic: Graph Theory
### Branch: Graph Structure and Traversal
- Vertex Degrees and the Handshake Lemma — degree sums, odd-degree vertices, regular graphs, acquaintance counts (~19)
- Connectivity and Distances — connected graphs, paths, distances, walks on road networks (~10)
- Trees and Labelled Graphs — trees, spanning trees, cycle rank, Cayley's formula, counting labelled graphs (~9)
- Directed Graphs and Tournaments — round-robin results, kings, score sequences, edge orientations, one-way road networks (~28)
- Eulerian Trails and Circuits — tracing figures in one stroke, Königsberg bridges, domino rings (~7)
- Hamiltonian Paths and Cycles — routes through every vertex, Icosian game, Dirac-type seatings (~8)

### Branch: Planarity, Colouring and Matching
- Planar Graphs — planarity, K5 and K3,3, embeddings on plane and torus (~5)
- Euler's Formula for Polyhedra — V−E+F; counting faces, edges and vertices of polyhedra and maps (~18)
- Graph Colouring — vertex, edge and map colouring; splitting vertex sets under degree conditions (~14)
- Bipartite Graphs and Matchings — bipartiteness, Hall's theorem, systems of distinct representatives, pairings (~8)

### Branch: Extremal Graph Theory
- Ramsey's Theorem — Ramsey numbers, monochromatic triangles in edge-coloured complete graphs (~25)
- Turán's Theorem and Triangle-Free Graphs — maximum edges without triangles or cliques; forced triangles (~13)
- Cliques and Independent Sets — forced cliques, people knowing everyone, independent sets in friendship graphs (~10)

## Topic: Extremal Combinatorics

- Zero-Sum and Divisible Subset Sums — subsets with sum divisible by n, Erdős–Ginzburg–Ziv type results (~14)

- Partitions of Integer Sets — splitting {1,...,n} or N into classes with sum or structure conditions (~18)

- Divisibility and Coprimality in Integer Subsets — chosen integers forcing divisor pairs or coprime pairs (~7)

### Branch: Designs and Number Arrays

### Branch: Chessboard and Grid Problems
- Non-Attacking Chess Pieces — maximal independent and dominating placements of rooks, bishops, queens, knights, kings (~39)

### Branch: Arrangements and Sequences

## Decisions and open questions
- Evidence: I read the first 100 characters of every member of all 78 clusters (3,226 questions) and allocated each cluster's members to subtopics; the allocations sum exactly to the cluster sizes. Mine: about 2,005 Combinatorics and 596 Geometry; about 625 belong elsewhere (Algebra ~275, Number Theory ~185, Probability ~85, others ~80).
- Combinatorics has six topics (Enumerative ~610, Graph Theory ~195, Extremal ~410, Combinatorial Geometry ~290, Games and Processes ~290, Logic and Algorithmic Puzzles ~235). That is more than the usual 1-3, but each is a recognised field and has 3-8 branches. Owner: if fewer are wanted, merge "Combinatorial Games and Processes" with "Logic and Algorithmic Puzzles" into one puzzle topic of six branches.
- Strategy versus content (Engel and other strategy books): invariants and monovariants, colouring arguments, the extremal principle, pigeonhole, double counting, induction, bijections, symmetry and strategy-stealing are tags, not nodes. I filed the problems by what they are about. Engel ch. 1 (invariance) goes to the two Processes branches, according to what changes (numbers on a blackboard, cells, lamps, pegs, tokens). Ch. 2 (colouring) goes to Tilings and Dissections and Colourings of the Plane and Space. Ch. 3 (extremal principle) goes to graph, point-set and set-family nodes. Ch. 4 (box principle) goes to Points in Bounded Regions, the Additive Combinatorics nodes and Lattice Points. Ch. 5 (enumerative combinatorics) goes to the Enumerative branches. Ch. 8 (games) goes to Two-Player Games.
- Pigeonhole Principle keeps one narrow subtopic (~13) for elementary "how many must be drawn to guarantee" questions, because it is a standard course section (Mladenovic 10.4) and a common search term. The other ~150 pigeonhole problems in the bank have a real content home, so they are filed there and tagged.
- Inclusion-Exclusion gets its own branch (with Derangements and Counting Surjections). The principle is a counting formula, closer to a theorem than a strategy. It is a whole Mladenovic chapter, and the quantity counted (a union, a set of permutations without fixed points, a set of onto maps) is the content.
- "Recurrence Relations for Counting" names a method. I kept it because of the fixed rule that Combinatorics sets up recurrences and Algebra solves them. Its scope is restricted to deriving a recursion for a counting sequence. Linear-recurrence solving in C244 (~17) goes to Algebra.
- Algorithmic and logic puzzles: I recommend a topic inside Combinatorics, not a separate subject. (1) SUBJECTS.md already lists logic, weighing and algorithmic puzzles under Combinatorics. (2) The ~235 questions are discrete reasoning about finite state spaces, information bounds and orderings, which is combinatorial content. (3) Genuine programming content is tiny: C245 (14, mostly A Practical Guide to Quantitative Finance and OpenQuant coding questions), plus 4 SQL queries in C374 and one C++ snippet. C245 is mapped to List and Array Algorithms. The 4 SQL queries go to the wastebasket as "Other: SQL". If computer-science sources are added later, an "Algorithms" subject would become worthwhile: List and Array Algorithms plus Sorting and Selection by Comparisons would seed it.
- "Puzzles" appears in the topic and three branch names because it is the field's own name (Levitin's "Algorithmic Puzzles"; SUBJECTS.md says "logic and weighing puzzles"). I kept it out of subtopic names.
- Counting versus existence pairs: a "how many" question goes to Enumerative Combinatorics; an existence, maximum or minimum question goes to the matching Extremal or Combinatorial Geometry node. The pairs are:
  - Counting Tilings of Strips / Domino and Polyomino Tilings
  - Counting Diagonals and Intersection Points / Diagonals and Triangulations of Polygons
  - Permutations with Restrictions / Number Arrangements with Adjacency Conditions
  - Counting Grid Fillings and Colourings / Number Tables with Row and Column Conditions / Colourings of Grids
  - Counting placements of chess pieces stays in Non-Attacking Chess Pieces (one home for the piece problems).
- Combinatorics/Geometry boundary: Combinatorial Geometry takes finite configurations, colourings of the plane, covering and packing arguments, tilings, lattice-point parity and topological arguments. Geometry takes metric and synthetic properties of specific figures, cross-sections and projections of convex solids, geometric inequalities and geometric optimisation. Euler's formula for polyhedra is graph content and goes to Graph Theory; metric questions about polyhedra go to Solid Geometry.
- Boundaries with other subjects:
  - Divisibility of binomial coefficients and factorials (Kummer, Lucas, Legendre; ~26 in C86) goes to Number Theory.
  - Counting integers with digit conditions stays here (~63). Digit-sum and base-representation properties go to Number Theory.
  - The Frobenius coin problem (C444), Diophantine approximation and Beatty sequences (C28), and integer-sided or Heronian figures solved as Diophantine equations (C113) go to Number Theory.
  - Probabilities computed by counting go to Probability (C314 and parts of others).
  - Rate, work and mixture word problems go to Algebra (C2, C380).
  - Trigonometric identities without a figure, roots of unity and inequalities in a single angle go to Algebra. Inequalities in the sides, medians or radii of a triangle stay in Geometry even when proved algebraically, following the fixed rule that Geometry owns geometric inequalities.
  - Geometric extremum problems solved only by derivatives (fencing a field, compost bins) go to Calculus. Synthetic ones (reflection, Fermat point, isoperimetric) stay in Geometric Optimisation.
- Wastebasket candidates: Winkler's US-geography trivia (~18, C374), pure riddles (~8: "two pairs of twins twice", the portrait riddle, the bookworm), and the 4 SQL queries.
- Small subtopics kept because they are core and the sources cover them: Circular Arrangements (7), Stirling Numbers and Set Partitions (6), Counting Surjections (5), Latin Squares (5), Chains, Antichains and Monotone Subsequences (5), Error-Correcting Codes (6), Non-Crossing Segments and Matchings (4), Tower of Hanoi (4), Voting and Social Choice (4; Arrow's theorem is Mladenovic 10.6), Planar Graphs (5), Power of a Point and Radical Axes (3), Altitudes and Orthocentre (4), Homothety and Spiral Similarity (4), Prisms and Pyramids (4), Nets and Unfoldings (3), Coordinate Geometry in Space (3).

Owner: confirm, or promote it to a topic if other groups contribute coordinate-geometry or conic questions.
