## Step 2. Analysis

### 2.1 Tags used (count / subjects spanned / definition)

PRINCIPLE family
- Conservation of Energy / First Law Energy Balance: 22 / 8 subjects (FM, HT, CET, MatSci, Elec, Mech, EM, waves). The solution writes an energy equation (Bernoulli, heat balance, power balance, work-energy, Griffith energy release, stored-energy equality) and solves it for the unknown.

### 2.3 Family verdicts

- Principle: marginal to useful. Only energy, mass, momentum, equilibrium and compatibility cross subjects, and they are broad: "Conservation of Energy" is in 22 of 104 questions (21 percent), too coarse as a filter on its own. Keep a small set; drop Kirchhoff and Angular Momentum.
- Model: marginal. Models mostly equal the subtopic name (Control Volume = FM and CET, Phasor = Elec). Exceptions that cross subjects: Equivalent Network, Linearisation, Element Decomposition, Symmetry.
- Tool: the most useful family. Optimisation, Ratio Method, Integration over Region, Orthogonal Decomposition, Iterative, Graphical, Linear System, Dimensional Analysis, Chain Rule appear across subjects and can be practised.
- Data: marginal. "Needs a table" is too generic; the specific chart is the subtopic.
- Task: mixed. Find Unknown, Derive and Explain are too frequent to discriminate, but Explain a Concept (no calculation), Compare Cases, Design or Size, Plot or Sketch and Find the Optimum change what the student does. This is the closest analogue of the owner's Point Finding.
- Other: mostly noise or solution style (Sanity Check is generated-solution boilerplate). Useful pieces: Unit Conversion, Empirical Correlation, Analogy, Design Trade-off, each with low counts.

### 2.4 Overall answer

Yes, but modestly. About a quarter of this sample has no cross-concept feature beyond its subtopic: single-formula plug-ins (18229 q = h dT, 4036 rated torque, 21053 Stefan-Boltzmann, 5983 pole-speed table) and recall questions (7141 caking coal, 7350 charcoal). For the rest, unifying features exist at two levels:
1. A small set of principles (energy, mass, momentum, equilibrium) across 5 to 8 subjects. Valid but coarse.
2. A medium set of tools and task archetypes (optimum, ratio from a known state, network analogy, orthogonal decomposition, symmetry, linearise, iterate, plot, compare cases, explain qualitatively) across 3 to 7 subjects, which a student would actually practise. These are the textbook equivalent of olympiad techniques, but weaker: textbook exercises are mostly "apply this chapter's formula", so most techniques are inseparable from the chapter. The better equivalents show up in advanced and proof-style questions (IIT papers, Gregory, Srinath, Stefanica, Kamal derivations) and in design/optimisation questions of the engineering books.

Expect only about 40 to 55 percent of the bank to receive any tag, and very few for Rajput/Nag/Callister short answers, or plug-in Kern, Som and Chapman exercises. "Point Finding" is the same kind of tag as Find the Optimum, Explain a Concept and Compare Cases: it names the task, not the topic.

Limits of this sample: 104 questions, no Dornbusch or Wilmott, many Claude-generated solutions whose style leaks into tags, and no subtopic labels, so "subjects spanned" is a coarser test than "subtopics spanned".

### 2.5 Candidate list (26 tags; apply only when the solution actually uses the feature)

Principle (5)
1. Conservation of Energy: an energy equation (heat, work, electrical, mechanical, stored) is the governing step.
2. Conservation of Mass or Continuity: mass or volume flow in equals out, or volume swept equals volume lost.
3. Conservation of Momentum: force equals rate of change of linear momentum (jets, control volumes, impacts).
4. Equilibrium of Forces or Moments: sums of forces or moments zero, including hydrostatic pressure balance.
5. Compatibility of Deformation: two parts constrained to share a displacement, twist or strain.

Model (5)
6. Equivalent Network (Resistance/Circuit Analogy): reduce the system to series-parallel resistances, circuits or an analogous network.
7. Steady-State Assumption: all time dependence set to zero, stated or implied.
8. Linearisation or Small Perturbation: expand about a known state, keep first order.
9. Element or Slice Decomposition: cut into thin strips, rings or elements, write the local law, integrate.
10. Symmetry Reduction: use symmetry to cancel terms or solve a fraction of the system.

Tool (9)
11. Find the Optimum: maximise or minimise a quantity (efficiency, variance, extent, best section).
12. Ratio from Known State: compute at a reference state and scale, constants cancelling.
13. Integration over Region or Volume (including Jacobian change of variables).
14. Orthogonal Decomposition: split into orthogonal parts so squares add, or project onto a basis.
15. Chain Rule or Change of Variables.
16. Linear System from Constraints: translate conditions to linear equations and solve or factor.
17. Iterative Solution: guess-and-update until convergence (Newton, friction factor, bootstrapping).
18. Graphical or Load-Line Solution: read the answer off an intersection or plotted curve.
19. Dimensional Analysis or Scaling: dimensionless groups or power laws used to scale between cases.

Task (5)
20. Explain a Concept: qualitative answer, no calculation required.
21. Compare Cases or Alternatives: evaluate two or more options and decide.
22. Design or Size to a Constraint: choose dimensions or numbers to meet a spec.
23. Plot or Sketch a Curve.
24. Point Finding (the owner's tag): find the specific points or boundary values where something is extreme or a condition changes.

Other (2)
25. Empirical Correlation or Table Lookup Required.
26. Unit or Scale Conversion Matters (K vs C, hp, rpm).

Not recommended as tags: Sanity Check (solution-writer artefact), Kirchhoff, Phasor, Control Volume, Free-Body Diagram, any single named theorem.
