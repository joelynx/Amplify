- **Reformulation** (8): split into **Graph Model**, **Translate to Linear Algebra**, **Reduce to Known Criterion**, **Probabilistic Interpretation**. Taggers will apply the unsplit tag to anything with a 'what the problem is really asking' paragraph, which is every solution in the corpus.
- **Construction** (11): split into **Explicit Construction** (exhibit example to prove existence) and **Bound and Construct** (counting lower bound matched by an example; 5 of 11 uses here: 11488, 11522, 11975, 12316, 17737). The second is a real archetype and very filterable.
- **Bounding** (8): split into **Size Estimate** (crude growth bound to cut cases) and **Squeeze**.
- **Decomposition** (5): split into **Orthogonal/Direct Decomposition** (algebra) and **Split Sum or Integral** (analysis).
- **Substitution** (8): split into **Change of Variables** (integrals, trig, symmetric forms) and **Algebraic Substitution** (rename expression to simplify).
- **Modular Arithmetic** (12): split into **Modular Arithmetic** (residue classes), **Order and Cycles of Powers** (Fermat, periodicity) and **Sieve by Modulus**.
- **Induction** (8): split Induction vs **Induction on Dimension** (linear algebra) vs **Strong Induction**; keep Infinite Descent separate.
- **Contradiction** (7): often a wrapper; keep, but require 'the contradiction is the main idea'.

### Is key vs uses worth storing?
Yes, with one caveat. In this sample key tags are what a student practises; uses tags are mostly supporting cast. Evidence: Casework is 4 key vs 10 uses, Bounding 2 vs 6, Parity 1 vs 4, Symmetry 2 vs 4, Pairing Terms 0 vs 3, Fermat's Little Theorem 0 vs 2, GCD-LCM Identity 0 vs 2, Bezout 1 vs 2, Telescoping 1 vs 2. Filtering on any mention would return mostly questions where casework or bounding is incidental; key-only gives a much cleaner set. Conversely Modular Arithmetic (12/0), Conditioning (4/0), Base Representation (3/0), Double Counting (6/3), Bijection (4/1) are almost always key, so for those the distinction costs nothing. Recommendation: store both, default the filter to key, and offer 'also appears' as an expand option. The caveat: a few real techniques (Fermat's Little Theorem, GCD-LCM identity, Pairing) only ever appear as uses; practising them via key-only gives an empty set, so such tags should be allowed to be promoted when they are the main idea of a smaller subproblem, or be listed as 'uses'-only secondary tags.

Do the families help? Partly. Families 1, 2, 3, 5 are real and match how a student thinks ('I want to practise counting'). Families 8 to 10 are subject buckets in disguise and duplicate the topic tree; they should be dropped and their tags dissolved into 4/5/6 or deleted. Families 4 and 6 are the weakest (Algebraic Moves is a grab-bag; Problem Transformation overlaps Reformulation). Recommend 6 families: Proof Methods, Counting Techniques, Number-Theoretic Moves (rename Divisibility and Residues), Algebraic Moves, Bounding and Estimation, Problem Transformation and Structure, with Geometry/Probability/Analysis ideas folded into those.
