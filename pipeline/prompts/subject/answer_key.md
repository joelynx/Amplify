- The answer key is read by a program: the quiz uses it to check the student's own answer, part by part. PART lines are plain text fields separated by bars. POINT lines are shown to the student and are LaTeX under the same rules as the rest of the reply: every symbol, subscript and formula inside \( \), never bare. Write one line per thing the student answers, in the order the question asks for them, each in one of these forms:
  PART <label> | NUMBER | <value> | <unit> | <what it is>
  PART <label> | NUMBER | <value> | <unit> | <what it is> | TOL <percent>
  PART <label> | CHOICE | <letter, or letters separated by commas>
  PART <label> | TRUEFALSE | <TRUE or FALSE>
  PART <label> | EXPRESSION | <formula in LaTeX, without delimiters>
  PART <label> | POINTS | <what it is>
  POINT <one thing a full-marks answer contains, in LaTeX>
- <label>: the part's label as the question prints it, without brackets (a, b, ii); 1, 2, 3 for the items of an unlabelled list of parts; a single dash - when the question has one thing to answer. Labels must all differ: when one part asks for several things, give each its own line with the part's label followed by a dot and a number (a.1, a.2; or 1, 2, 3 when the question has no parts of its own, never -.1).
- NUMBER: for every numerical result the question asks for. <value> is a plain number with no unit, no thousands separators and no LaTeX (38.6, 0.0213, 1.25e6), rounded as in your Final answer line. <unit> is the unit that value is in, in plain ASCII: {{UNITS}}; write percent for a percentage and a single dash - for a pure number. Use the unit the question asks for or states its data in. Add TOL only when the result depends on reading a chart or on property tables whose editions differ noticeably, as the relative tolerance in percent (TOL 3, at most 50); otherwise leave it out. A result known only to its order of magnitude is a POINTS part, not a NUMBER.
- <what it is>: a few plain words naming what is asked (thermal efficiency of the reheat cycle). Plain ASCII words only: no LaTeX, no symbols, and never the answer or a value from the solution. It may be left empty after the bar when the label already says enough, as for a multiple-choice question.
- CHOICE: the correct option's letter, counting the options in the order printed and starting from a, whether the question prints them as (a), (b) or as a numbered list. When the question asks for every correct option, give all their letters separated by commas. Use CHOICE only when the question prints its options as a list or with letters; when it names the candidates inside a sentence (pick the ones that ...), the answer is a POINTS part naming the right ones.
- TRUEFALSE: for a statement the student marks true or false.
- EXPRESSION: when the answer is a formula rather than a number.
- POINTS: for every part with no single answer (explain, describe, compare, derive, draw, list): the PART line followed by 3 to 8 POINT lines, each one thing a full-marks answer must contain, one short sentence concrete enough that the student can tick it off against their own answer. For a sketch, the features the sketch must show; for a derivation, the key steps and the result.
- Every part of the question appears in the key, and the key agrees with your Final answer line.
